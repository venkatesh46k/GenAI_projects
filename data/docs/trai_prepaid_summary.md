# TRAI Regulations Relevant to Prepaid Billing (Summary)

This is a plain-language summary of publicly known Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) rules that affect how prepaid plans are offered and billed. It is a simplified operational summary, not legal text.

## Plan validity

- Operators must offer at least one plan voucher with a validity of 30 days, and validity is counted from the date of recharge (a 30-day plan is valid for 30 days, not for a calendar month). In practice operators also offer plans of 28 days, and subscribers should always check the stated validity.
- Special tariff vouchers and combo vouchers may have any validity, but the validity must be stated clearly before purchase.
- Operators offer plan vouchers with a validity of up to 365 days.

## Transparency

- Tariff details, including price, validity, data, voice and SMS benefits, must be clearly published and available to subscribers before purchase.
- Charges must not be deducted from the balance without the subscriber's consent, except for usage at published tariffs.
- Subscribers are entitled to check their balance and usage and to request an itemised record of usage.

## Balance and lapsing

- The main balance (talk-time value) is not to be forfeited arbitrarily while a subscriber has an active plan. Rules about what happens to the balance after validity ends are in the plan validity document.
- After a line has no activity for an extended period, an operator may disconnect the number and reallocate it, but only after giving advance notice.

## Consumer protection

- Operators must have a complaint redressal system. A subscriber who is unhappy with the response can escalate within the operator's grievance structure and, thereafter, to the appellate authority.
- Subscribers must be informed of complaint status and resolution.
- Refunds for wrong charges should be credited without undue delay.

## SMS and calls

- Operators must offer at least a minimum number of free SMS per day in bundled packs as stated in the plan.
- Unsolicited promotional communication is regulated under the Do Not Disturb (DND) preferences.

Where the operator's own policy in the other documents is more subscriber-friendly than this summary, the operator's policy applies.
